*University fees, accommodation, food, scholarships and the money you should plan before leaving India.*

Imagine getting admission to an Italian university, receiving your visa and finally arriving in Europe.

It sounds exciting. But before booking your flight, there is one question every student and family needs to answer:

**How much does it actually cost to study and live in Italy?**

If you are applying from India, you probably want to understand more than just university tuition fees. You need to know how much rent costs, what groceries will cost, how much money you should keep for transportation, whether scholarships can reduce your expenses and how much financial proof is required for your student visa.

The answer is not the same for everyone.

Living in Milan is very different from living in Messina, Palermo or Perugia. A student staying in a private studio will have a different budget from someone sharing an apartment or receiving subsidised accommodation.

This guide brings together official information, published accommodation data and practical student budgeting examples to help Indian students plan their finances before moving to Italy.

## 1. How Much Does It Cost to Study in Italy?

Italy can be a relatively affordable European study destination, particularly when compared with some other popular international education destinations. However, your actual cost depends on your university, course, city, accommodation and lifestyle.

The European Commission provides the following general tuition and living-cost guidance for Italy. [[1]]

| Expense | General guideline |
|---|---:|
| Public university tuition | €900–€4,000 per year |
| Private institution tuition | €6,000–€20,000+ per year |
| Monthly living expenses | €700–€1,100 |

These are broad planning figures, not fixed prices for every university or student.

Some public universities offer tuition reductions or exemptions based on financial circumstances and their own regulations. Your actual tuition fee must be checked against the official fee schedule for your chosen academic year.

### What should an Indian student budget for?

Think about your expenses in three categories:

**Before departure:** University application and enrolment expenses, document preparation, visa application, flight tickets and initial accommodation.

**Regular expenses:** Rent, groceries, utilities, transportation, phone and personal spending.

**Academic and administrative expenses:** Tuition, health insurance, residence permit procedures, study materials and other university-related charges.

Keeping these categories separate makes it easier to understand how much money you need before leaving India and how much you need every month after arriving.

## 2. Public vs Private Universities: Understanding Tuition Fees

### Public universities

Public universities are a popular choice for international students because their tuition fees are generally lower than those of private institutions.

However, public university fees are not necessarily the same across Italy.

The amount may depend on:

- Your university and selected programme.
- The applicable tuition regulations.
- Your financial documentation.
- Your student category.
- Available reductions and exemptions.

Some universities use family-income documentation to determine tuition fees. International students may need additional documentation from their home country, depending on the university's requirements.

### Private universities

Private institutions generally have higher tuition fees, although scholarships and financial assistance may be available.

Before selecting a university, compare the complete cost of studying there, including accommodation, transport and other living expenses.

**Tip for Indian students:** Do not select a university based only on the lowest advertised tuition fee. Compare the academic programme, city, accommodation options and total annual budget.

## 3. Accommodation in Italy: Which Cities Are More Affordable?

Accommodation is often one of the biggest expenses for international students.

Most students consider one of the following options:

- A private room in a shared apartment.
- A shared bedroom.
- A private studio.
- University or regional student accommodation.
- Private student residences.

For many students, renting a room in a shared apartment is a practical starting point.

### Average advertised room rents across Italian university cities

Immobiliare.it Insights published a nationwide analysis of single-room rental prices in August 2026. Its figures show substantial differences between university cities. [[2]]

| City | Average advertised single-room rent |
|---|---:|
| Milan | €704/month |
| Florence | €627/month |
| Rome | €615/month |
| Bologna | €585/month |
| Padua | €496/month |
| Turin | €479/month |
| Naples | €447/month |
| Palermo | €315/month |
| Messina | €285/month |
| Catania | €284/month |
| Perugia | €301/month |
| Pisa | €364/month |

These are advertised rental averages from the report, not guaranteed prices or actual signed-contract prices. Utilities may be additional.

The difference is significant.

A student looking for a room in Milan may face a very different housing budget from someone studying in Messina or Palermo.

### What should you check before renting?

Do not look at the monthly rent alone.

Ask whether the following are included:

- Electricity.
- Gas.
- Water.
- Internet.
- Heating.
- Building or condominium charges.

Also check the security deposit, contract duration, registration, location and distance from your university.

A room advertised at €350 may not necessarily be cheaper than one advertised at €400 if the first has substantial additional charges.

## 4. Food and Grocery Expenses: What Should Indian Students Expect?

Food is another important part of your monthly budget.

Italy has supermarkets, local markets, discount grocery stores, small neighbourhood shops and university canteens.

If you cook regularly, you can manage your grocery spending more effectively than if you eat at restaurants every day.

### Common grocery purchases

- Rice and pasta.
- Vegetables and seasonal fruits.
- Milk, eggs and bread.
- Lentils and other protein sources.
- Cooking oil and spices.
- Meat, fish or other protein.
- Cleaning products and household essentials.

### Can Indian students find Indian groceries?

Yes, Indian and international grocery shops exist in several Italian cities, although their availability depends on where you live.

You may find products such as:

- Basmati rice.
- Dal and lentils.
- Indian spices.
- Atta and other flour.
- Pickles and packaged foods.

However, prices and availability can vary. It is useful to locate nearby international grocery stores before choosing accommodation.

### A practical way to save money

Plan your meals for the week, compare supermarket prices and avoid buying unnecessary items every day.

Cooking at home can make a meaningful difference over an entire academic year.

## 5. Transportation: Do Students Need a Monthly Travel Pass?

Italy has different public transportation systems depending on the city.

Students may use:

- Buses.
- Trams.
- Metro systems.
- Regional trains.
- Bicycle-sharing services.

Some universities negotiate discounted transport arrangements for their students.

For example, the University of Messina has published a discounted annual bus and tram pass for eligible students. Other cities have their own student transport arrangements. [[3]]

Before selecting accommodation, check:

1. How far the university campus is.
2. Whether public transport reaches your neighbourhood.
3. The cost of student passes.
4. Whether your route operates during evenings and weekends.
5. Whether you can walk or cycle to campus.

Remember that transport costs are not identical across Italy.

## 6. Health Insurance and Other Medical Expenses

Health coverage is an important part of preparing to study in Italy.

International students need to arrange suitable health coverage according to the applicable visa and residence requirements.

Depending on their circumstances, non-EU students may use qualifying private insurance or investigate registration with Italy's National Health Service (SSN). [[1]]

When preparing your budget, consider:

- Health insurance.
- Medical appointments.
- Prescription medicines.
- Dental expenses.
- Services not covered by your insurance.

Read the insurance policy carefully before purchasing it. Check the duration, exclusions, coverage limits and requirements for your intended stay.

## 7. Visa, Residence Permit and Initial Documentation Costs

For Indian students, the visa process involves more than submitting an admission letter.

Depending on your application, you may need to prepare:

- University admission or pre-enrolment documentation.
- Financial evidence.
- Accommodation information.
- Health insurance.
- Academic documents.
- Translations and legalisation, where required.
- Visa application fees and service charges.
- Residence permit documentation after arrival.

The exact requirements depend on the applicable visa instructions and your individual circumstances.

### How much money must Indian students show for an Italian student visa?

This is one of the most important financial questions.

For academic years 2026–27 and 2027–28, the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs states that applicants for a university-enrolment study visa must demonstrate minimum financial resources of **€10,179.85 for the first year**. The return-ticket cost must also be considered. [[4]]

At the European Central Bank reference exchange rate of 12 November 2026, €1 was approximately ₹104.21. This makes €10,179.85 approximately **₹10.61 lakh**. [[5]]

| Financial reference | Approximate amount |
|---|---:|
| Required annual financial means | €10,179.85 |
| Approximate INR equivalent | ₹10.61 lakh |

**Important:** This is a financial-means requirement for the visa process. It is not a fixed monthly spending allowance, university tuition fee or guarantee of visa approval.

The actual documents accepted as financial evidence must follow the Italian Ministry's rules and the instructions of the consulate responsible for your application.

Do not assume that a bank guarantee or an informal financial promise will be accepted.

## 8. Scholarships in Italy: An Important Opportunity for Indian Students

One reason many international students consider Italy is the availability of regional and university-based financial assistance.

Regional student-support programmes are particularly important to investigate.

Depending on the region, the annual scholarship competition and the student's eligibility, benefits may include:

- Financial assistance.
- Accommodation services.
- Subsidised or free meals.
- Tuition-related benefits.
- Other student-support services.

### What are regional scholarships?

Italy has regional organisations responsible for providing student-support services.

Some examples include:

| Region | Student-support organisation |
|---|---|
| Lombardy | Regional DSU services |
| Emilia-Romagna | ER.GO |
| Piedmont | EDISU Piemonte |
| Tuscany | DSU Toscana |
| Lazio | DiSCo Lazio |
| Sicily | ERSU organisations |

Each organisation has its own annual competition, documentation requirements, deadlines and benefit structure.

For example, EDISU Piemonte publishes specific application procedures for international students, including consular documentation requirements. ERSU Palermo also publishes guidance on income and asset documentation for foreign applicants. [[6]] [[7]]

### Can Indian students apply for regional scholarships?

Indian students may be eligible to apply for regional benefits when they meet the relevant conditions.

However, international applicants should pay particular attention to financial documentation.

Depending on the regional competition, students may need documents relating to:

- Family composition.
- Family income.
- Property and assets.
- Financial circumstances in India.
- Official translations into Italian.
- Legalisation or other formal authentication.

The exact list depends on the regional scholarship notice.

### Does a scholarship guarantee free education?

No.

A scholarship may provide financial support, accommodation, meals or tuition-related benefits, but the package depends on the specific competition and the student's position in the ranking.

Eligibility, selection and actual payment are different stages.

Never plan your entire financial future around an award before you have received the official result and understood its payment conditions.

## 9. How Much Should an Indian Student Budget Each Month?

Let's create three illustrative monthly budgets.

These are planning scenarios, not official national averages or guaranteed spending levels.

| Expense | Lower-cost scenario | Moderate scenario | Higher-cost scenario |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Accommodation | €285 | €450 | €700 |
| Food and groceries | €180 | €230 | €300 |
| Utilities and internet | €40 | €60 | €90 |
| Transportation | €20 | €35 | €50 |
| Phone and personal expenses | €55 | €85 | €120 |
| **Monthly total** | **€580** | **€860** | **€1,260** |

The accommodation figures are illustrative. The lower-cost example uses the approximate advertised single-room price reported for Messina in August 2026. It does not mean that every student can find a suitable room at that price.

The other figures are budgeting assumptions, not published city averages.

These scenarios exclude annual tuition, flights, visa and residence permit costs, insurance, housing deposits, study materials and emergency savings.

### What does that mean in Indian rupees?

Using the ECB reference rate of 12 November 2026, approximately €1 = ₹104.21:

| Monthly budget | Approximate INR |
|---|---:|
| €580 | ₹60,400 |
| €860 | ₹89,600 |
| €1,260 | ₹1,31,300 |

Exchange rates change, and banks or remittance services may apply different rates and charges. These INR values are only reference conversions.

### A real student example from Messina

One international student currently living in Messina reports spending approximately €440 per month, including €250 for room rent.

That is a personal example, not an average for Italy or even a guaranteed budget for Messina.

It demonstrates that individual spending can be lower than general planning estimates, especially when accommodation and lifestyle costs are carefully managed.

However, students should not assume they can reproduce the same budget in every city.

## 10. First-Year Budget: What Should You Prepare Before Leaving India?

Your first year may involve expenses that do not repeat every month.

Consider these separately:

| Expense category | What to include |
|---|---|
| University | Tuition and enrolment charges |
| Visa | Application and service fees |
| Travel | Flight and local transport |
| Accommodation | Deposit, advance rent and setup |
| Documentation | Translation and legalisation |
| Healthcare | Insurance or applicable registration |
| Daily life | Food, utilities and transport |
| Emergency reserve | Unexpected expenses |

A useful calculation is:

**First-year budget = Annual tuition + (Monthly living expenses × 12) + Initial arrival expenses + Insurance and administrative costs + Emergency savings**

Do not confuse the amount needed to demonstrate financial means for the visa with the full cost of your first year.

Prepare a separate financial plan for both.

## 11. Can Indian Students Work Part-Time in Italy?

International students with an appropriate residence permit may generally work up to 20 hours per week, subject to a maximum of 1,040 hours per year. [[1]]

However, finding employment is not guaranteed.

Opportunities depend on:

- Italian language ability.
- Your city.
- Your university timetable.
- Previous experience.
- Employer requirements.
- Local job availability.

Part-time work may help with everyday expenses and provide useful experience, but it should not be treated as guaranteed funding for tuition, rent or visa requirements.

It is better to arrive with a realistic financial plan that does not depend on immediately finding a job.

## 12. Seven Ways to Manage Your Money in Italy

**1. Compare cities before choosing your university.**

Room rents can differ dramatically. Compare accommodation and transport alongside university fees.

**2. Start looking for accommodation early.**

Do not wait until your flight is booked to begin researching rooms.

**3. Investigate regional scholarships.**

Read the latest official competition notices and prepare your Indian financial documentation early.

**4. Cook at home.**

Plan groceries and prepare regular meals rather than depending on restaurants.

**5. Use student discounts.**

Check your university's transport, canteen and other student-service arrangements.

**6. Keep an emergency reserve.**

Initial deposits, medical needs and unexpected expenses can disrupt even a carefully prepared budget.

**7. Track your expenses in euros and rupees.**

Keep an eye on exchange rates, transfer fees and your actual monthly spending.

## 13. Final Thoughts: Is Italy Affordable for Indian Students?

Italy can be a financially realistic option for Indian students, but the answer depends on the university, city, accommodation and available financial support.

Public universities offer a wide range of programmes, and eligible international students can investigate regional and university scholarship opportunities.

However, it is important to plan beyond tuition fees.

A student in Milan may face a very different accommodation budget from someone in Messina or Palermo. A scholarship may reduce expenses, but the award and payment timing should never be assumed.

The smartest approach is to compare the complete financial picture before selecting your university.

**Do not ask only, "How much does Italy cost?" Ask, "Which Italian university and city fit my academic goals and my family's budget?"**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Italy affordable for Indian students?

It can be, depending on the university, city, accommodation and financial support available. Compare your full annual budget before making a decision.

### How much does it cost to study in Italy for Indian students?

Public university tuition is generally listed at €900–€4,000 per year as a broad guideline, while living expenses vary by city. The European Commission gives a general living-cost range of €700–€1,100 per month. [[1]]

### Which Italian cities have cheaper student accommodation?

The August 2026 advertised single-room rental survey reported lower average asking prices in cities such as Messina, Catania, Palermo, Perugia and Foggia compared with Milan, Florence and Rome. Actual availability and rent depend on the property. [[2]]

### Can Indian students get scholarships in Italy?

Indian students can investigate regional and university scholarships, subject to the relevant income, academic and documentation requirements.

### Is a regional scholarship available in every Italian city?

Regional student-support systems operate across Italy, but the responsible organisation, benefits and application rules depend on the university's region.

### How much money is required for an Italian student visa in 2026–27?

The Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs specifies €10,179.85 in annual financial means for university-enrolment study visa applications for 2026–27 and 2027–28, with the return-ticket cost also considered. [[4]]

### Can I work while studying in Italy?

Students with an appropriate study residence permit may generally work up to 20 hours per week and 1,040 hours per year, subject to applicable rules. [[1]]

### Can a scholarship cover accommodation and food?

Some regional programmes offer accommodation, meal services or financial assistance. The actual benefits depend on the specific scholarship competition and eligibility.

### Should I choose Milan or a smaller city?

It depends on your programme, academic goals, budget and available accommodation. Compare the complete cost of living, not just the university tuition.
